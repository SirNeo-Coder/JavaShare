Discussion

  • Look at line 8 of index.html. <nav> holds the navigation bar: a group of links at the top of the page.
  • Look at line 8 of index.html. <a href="index.html">Home</a> is a link. a makes it, href names the page to open, and the words inside, Home, are the link text.
  • Look at line 8 of index.html. <a href="projects.html">Projects</a> opens projects.html, the second page of this project.
  • Look at line 8 of index.html. <a href="contact.html">Contact</a> points to contact.html, which does not exist yet: Step 2 makes it.
  • Look at line 8 of index.html. </a> | <a the bars between the links are plain text, so the output shows Home | Projects | Contact. The href values are not page text.
  • Look at line 9 of index.html. <h1>Juan Dela Cruz</h1> is Week 2's heading, now under the navigation bar.
  • Look at line 12 of index.html. a senior high school student in the <em>ICT strand</em>.</p> ends Week 1's introduction.
  • Look at line 5 of projects.html. <title>Projects of Juan Dela Cruz</title> gives the second page its own tab title.
  • Look at line 8 of projects.html. <h1>Projects of Juan Dela Cruz</h1> is the second page's heading; its list on lines 10 to 13 is a Week 1 ul.
  • The output ends with Media checks:, one line per picture, sound or video: whether the file loaded. Media are not page text.
