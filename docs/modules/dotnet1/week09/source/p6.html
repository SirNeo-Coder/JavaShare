Performance task · My Resume, Week 9: Flexbox and a Media Query · 50 points

Start Thursday; finish and present Friday. In CodeShare open Projects, then Personal performance-task project. Your Week 8 site carries forward: add this week's rules to your own styles.css.

Turn at least two boxes of your own site into flex containers: your skills list, your gallery, your nav bar or your form. On each one write display: flex, choose flex-direction, and set the spacing with gap instead of a margin on every item; add flex-wrap: wrap to any row that may run out of width. Then add at least one media query, @media (max-width: 900px), that makes your layout better on a narrow page: stack your nav items, or turn a column of cards into a wrapping row. Nothing inside the query may add, remove or re-letter a word: your output must be the same at every width. Your own project's page fills the page window, so whether your query is on depends on your browser window's width: make it narrower if the query is off, wider if it is already on. The page window follows the browser window and cannot be dragged on its own. Flexbox and media queries work the same online and offline; offline your gallery pictures still show only their alt text until you add them online.

Use only safe details: no home address, phone number, birth date, ID numbers or personal social-media accounts, and no other people in any picture. Run code until index.html runs with no error, then press Submit this version. Saving or running is not submitting.

Prepare for these questions

  1. What does display: flex do to a box's children?
  2. Why is gap easier to change than a margin on each item?
  3. What does your media query change, and at which width?
  4. Why must a media query never change the words on the page?
  5. Which of your rows needs flex-wrap?
